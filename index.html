Practice 01 · Season kickoff

Back on the field.

A fast-moving first practice to find the throwers, dependable hands, route runners, and defenders—then see who can read the play cards under pressure.

4th grade 90 minutes No flags: two-hand touch Water every 20 minutes

Ready

The practice plan

Keep the lines short, keep the ball moving, and make quick notes instead of stopping every rep to coach.

0:00

0:00–0:08 · 8 minutes

Welcome + dynamic warmup

Set the tone: hustle between drills, encourage teammates, and listen when a coach speaks.

  • Easy jog, high knees, butt kicks
  • Side shuffle, backpedal, carioca
  • Walking lunges
  • Three progressive 15-yard accelerations
No-flags ruleTwo-hand touch between the shoulders and waist. No diving, pushing, or unnecessary contact.
0:08

0:08–0:18 · 10 minutes

Movement + conditioning circuit

Set four cones in a 10–15-yard square. Run three or four short rounds with about 30 seconds of recovery.

  1. Sprint out, backpedal back
  2. Shuffle right and left
  3. Sprint, plant, and change direction
  4. Ball-carry relay with a clean handoff
Watch forEffort, speed, balance, coordination, and who naturally competes.
0:18

0:18–0:30 · 12 minutes

Partner throwing + catching

Pair players 5–7 yards apart. Increase the distance for players who are comfortable.

  • Five chest-level throws
  • Five throws to each side
  • Five catches moving forward
  • Five catches moving sideways
Coach it quicklyShow a target. Step toward the throw. Watch the ball into the hands. Secure it before turning upfield.
0:30

0:30–0:43 · 13 minutes

QB + receiver evaluation

Use two passing lines. Everyone gets several turns at quarterback and receiver.

  • Quick slant
  • Five-yard out
  • Short curl
  • Go route
EvaluateAccuracy, arm strength, decision speed, catching consistency, route running, and adjustment to the ball.
0:43

0:43–0:53 · 10 minutes

Man-coverage challenge

Pair one receiver with one defender. Coach drops back 8–10 yards as quarterback. The receiver runs a real or invented route within 10–15 yards.

  • Throw when the receiver separates—or challenge tight coverage
  • Rotate matchups regularly
  • Everyone plays receiver and defender
  • Catch = offense point; incompletion, breakup, or pick = defense point
  • Play to three, then switch partners
Defensive cuesLine up slightly inside. Watch the hips. Don’t guess at the first move. Stay close without grabbing. When the ball comes, get a hand up and play through the hands.
0:53

0:53–1:05 · 12 minutes

Play-card review

  1. Players identify their positions
  2. Walk through the formation
  3. Run it at half speed without defenders
  4. Run it full speed against cones or coaches
  5. Rotate positions and repeat
Ask before the snapWhat position are you? Where do you line up? What is your first job? Where should the open space appear?
1:05

1:05–1:25 · 20 minutes

Controlled scrimmage

  • Six-play offensive possessions
  • Coach calls or shows the play card
  • Rotate quarterbacks and primary receivers
  • No rush for the first few possessions
  • Add a five-second rush count later
  • Include third-and-short, goal line, and one final-play situation
Keep it movingCorrect alignment or assignment mistakes briefly. Let ordinary incompletions and dropped passes go.
Watch forCommunication, spacing, play-card knowledge, defensive awareness, response to mistakes, and decisions under pressure.
1:25

1:25–1:30 · 5 minutes

Finish + team huddle

Finish with a team relay or “catch it to leave practice” round.

  • What did we do well?
  • What should we improve next practice?
  • Who saw a teammate do something helpful?
